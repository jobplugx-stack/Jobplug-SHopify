Design and client journey

The redesign keeps the brand’s blue and introduces warm neutral backgrounds, consistent typography, clear service categories, and visible booking/intake actions. The journey is: understand the support → choose a service or package → book a consultation or complete intake → purchase through Shopify → follow onboarding.

Navigation: Services, Packages, Success stories, Our story, Book a consultation. The footer adds career resources, intake, contact, and cart. Existing product, collection, page, and blog paths are preserved. Google Forms handles intake submissions directly; the theme does not intercept answers or write to an imitation form endpoint.

What is ready

  • Native Shopify Online Store 2.0 theme: homepage sections, product, collection, page, blog, article, cart, search, 404, and app-block support.
  • Existing Shopify prices are read dynamically in the theme. Preview prices are the captured catalog snapshot.
  • Responsive service filters, mobile menu, accessible focus states, skip link, FAQs, and native product/contact forms.
  • Calendly and Google Form URLs are editable in Theme settings → Booking and intake.
  • Raw HTML, clean main content, product/collection JSON, sitemap files, and downloaded image assets are retained.

Review before launch

Payment terms conflict

The Plug-In Plan’s checkout product is $100, but its copy says a $99 deposit, $300 balance, and $399 total. The package collection also displays $400. Confirm the actual deposit, total, balance due date, and conditions before enabling the deposit purchase option. The draft sends visitors to a consultation for this plan.

Product links conflict with labels

The old homepage’s Mock Technical Interview link goes to the product now titled The Plug-In Plan. A 30-Day Tech Interview feature links to the $20 Technical Interview Preparation product. The redesign routes these services by actual catalog content.

All testimonial author labels conflict

The first account identifies itself as Tolu but its author label is Zeke. The second identifies Wale but is labeled Terry. The third describes Wale but is labeled Thomas. The redesign uses one short anonymized quote and source-grounded summaries; confirm names, permission, employers, dates, and outcomes before adding named case studies.

Unsubstantiated counters and logos

The original homepage source contains 13 hiring partners, 25 technologies, 1K placements, and 2K applications, but the counter text does not reliably render. Company logos are presented under a combined interviews-and-jobs claim. These figures and implied company affiliations are not independently supported by the pages and are omitted from the draft.

Intake copy is stale

The old intake page promises fewer than two minutes and four quick questions. The live linked form is a longer career onboarding form; its current introduction says about 10 minutes. The draft avoids the shorter promise and preserves the actual Google Form.

Unrelated template content

The old intake page includes music-themed filler, Latin placeholder text, and a generic team roster. This content is archived and removed from the redesign.

Consultation duration

The Calendly URL ends in /30min, but the live event is titled 15 Minute Consultation and displays 15 min. The draft follows the live event.

Template errors

The old footer includes a Liquid image-width error, and testimonial CSS contains invalid mask-image Liquid URLs. The new theme passes Shopify Theme Check without offenses.

Deployment and integration verification

The current CLI identity cannot access 0mdj10-0h.myshopify.com. No live theme, products, orders, form settings, or responses were changed. The Google Form and Calendly both load at their direct URLs; the Google embed also loads on the existing public store. Their frames remain blank in the local browser preview. Repeat embedded checks on an unpublished Shopify theme and verify a test response in the owner’s Google Forms Responses tab before launch. No test response or appointment was submitted.

How a Professionally Edited Resume Can Land You More Interviews
 Feb 24 
Introduction
Your resume is your first impression on potential employers. A well-crafted resume can significantly increase your chances of getting interviews, while a poorly structured one can cost you opportunities.
The Importance of a Professional Resume
Recruiters spend an average of 6-7 seconds scanning a resume before deciding whether to read further. A professional resume editor ensures that:
Your skills and experience are highlighted effectively.
The format is clean and easy to read.
Keywords match the job description, increasing your chances of passing applicant tracking systems (ATS).
Common Resume Mistakes
Many job seekers make errors such as:
Too much or too little information – A balance is crucial.
Generic job descriptions – Instead, use quantifiable achievements.
Poor formatting – A disorganized layout can hurt readability.
How Resume Editing Helps
A resume editor refines your document, ensuring it aligns with industry standards and showcases your value. This small investment can lead to faster job placement and higher salary offers.
Final Thoughts
A well-edited resume can be the key to unlocking new job opportunities. Invest in a professional review to maximize your career potential.

Salary Negotiation: How to Get Paid What You Deserve
 Feb 24 
Introduction
Many job seekers hesitate to negotiate salary, fearing they’ll lose the offer. However, negotiating your salary is a critical skill that can significantly impact your career earnings.
Why Salary Negotiation Matters
A higher starting salary means greater long-term earnings.
Many employers expect candidates to negotiate.
Negotiating demonstrates confidence and professionalism.
Salary Negotiation Tips
Know Your Worth – Research salary benchmarks on Glassdoor and LinkedIn.
Let the Employer Make the First Offer – This gives you leverage.
Prepare Your Counteroffer – Highlight your skills and market demand.
Consider Total Compensation – Look at benefits, bonuses, and growth potential.
Final Thoughts
Negotiating your salary can lead to higher pay, better job satisfaction, and financial security. Don’t leave money on the table—ask for what you’re worth!

Why Technical Interview Preparation is the Key to Landing Your Dream Job
 Feb 24 
Introduction
Technical interviews can be intimidating, especially for professionals in software development, data science, and engineering. Proper preparation is the key to success.
Common Technical Interview Challenges
Candidates often struggle with:
Algorithm and coding problems – Companies like Google and Amazon prioritize these.
System design questions – Essential for senior roles.
Behavioral questions – Often overlooked but equally important.
How to Prepare for Technical Interviews
Master Data Structures & Algorithms – Practice on platforms like LeetCode and HackerRank.
Understand System Design – Study architectural patterns and real-world use cases.
Mock Interviews – Practice under real interview conditions.
Review Past Projects – Be ready to discuss your experience in depth.
Final Thoughts
A structured approach to technical interview preparation can increase your confidence, improve your problem-solving skills, and help you stand out from the competition.
